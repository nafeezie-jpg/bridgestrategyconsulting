The Idea

The Last Mile

The world already grows enough food, and it already knows how to farm. Hunger and rural poverty survive somewhere quieter, in the last mile between what institutions produce and what actually reaches the farmer.

That distance is where food security is decided, and few people have worked it from every side. I have. I have trained smallholder farmers as an extension officer, moved fertilizer at national scale, built technology meant to cross the gap, evaluated the aid designed to close it, and carried what I found back into the rooms where the decisions are made, from government ministries to the institutions that finance food systems.

My doctoral research named one version of the problem. Aid can lift a country's exports while quietly raising the cost of trading, so the gains register at the institution and never reach the people who grew the goods. The pattern repeats wherever food moves. The work, every time, is to close the distance.

I am an agricultural policy and food systems strategist. That range taught me to speak to the farmer and the funder in the same afternoon, in language each one trusts, and that fluency is the whole job.

Research

The Aid-for-Trade Paradox

My doctoral research named and evidenced a paradox at the heart of aid policy, building an original dataset from scratch and combining a Theory of Change evaluation with econometric modeling. The finding was that aid can improve a country's export performance while raising its trade costs, with direct implications for how development finance is targeted and measured.
University of East Anglia · 2025

Point of View

What I argue.

The positions my work is built on, applied to the questions food systems are facing right now.

01
The world grows enough. The food dies on the way to market.
Up to half of some harvests are lost after picking, to broken roads, poor storage, and missing cold chains. The crisis lives in delivery, in the stretch between the farm and the market.
02
Aid lifts a nation's exports while the farmer who grew the crop stays poor.
My doctoral research evidenced this paradox along agricultural trade corridors. Development finance is measured at the port and felt least in the field, and closing that gap is the real test of whether aid works.
03
Inputs and extension are the cheapest technology in agriculture, and the most neglected.
Getting the right fertilizer, seed, and advice to a farmer changes more than any single innovation does on its own. Rebuilding that delivery is how a food system turns what it already knows into what farmers actually do.
04
Food security is decided in the last mile, where the institution meets the household.
Everything upstream, the research, the funding, the policy, only matters if it survives the final stretch to the family it was meant for. That stretch is my whole subject.
